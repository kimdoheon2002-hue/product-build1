Korean Greetings 101: How and When to Use Them

Beginner guide · 5 min read

If you've studied even a little Korean, you already know 안녕하세요 (annyeonghaseyo). It's usually the first phrase every textbook teaches, and for good reason — it works in almost any polite situation. But real spoken Korean has more nuance than a single "hello," and knowing which greeting fits which situation will make you sound far more natural.

안녕하세요 — the safe, all-purpose greeting

안녕하세요 is polite and neutral. You can use it with strangers, coworkers, shopkeepers, teachers, or anyone older than you. It works at any time of day — Korean doesn't have separate words for "good morning" and "good evening" the way English does.

선생님, 안녕하세요.
Hello, teacher.

안녕 — casual, for close friends only

Drop the polite ending and you get 안녕 (annyeong), a casual greeting used only between close friends or people younger than you who you're already familiar with. Using 안녕 with a stranger or someone older can come across as rude, so when in doubt, stick with 안녕하세요.

Time-specific greetings do exist, but they're less common

You may also hear 좋은 아침이에요 ("good morning") or 잘 자요 ("good night, sleep well"), but these are used far less often than in English. 안녕하세요 covers almost every daytime greeting, and 잘 자요 is reserved for close friends or family right before bed.

Saying goodbye depends on who is leaving

Korean has two different goodbyes depending on who is staying and who is going:

  • 안녕히 가세요 — said to the person who is leaving, while you stay.
  • 안녕히 계세요 — said by the person who is leaving, to the person who stays.

For example, if you're leaving a shop and the shopkeeper is staying, you say 안녕히 계세요 to them, and they'll reply with 안녕히 가세요 to you.

A simple rule for beginners

Until you're comfortable reading the social situation, default to the polite forms — 안녕하세요 for hello, 안녕히 가세요 / 안녕히 계세요 for goodbye. Koreans are generally understanding toward learners, but starting polite and relaxing later is always safer than the reverse.
