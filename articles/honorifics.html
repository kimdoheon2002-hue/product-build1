Understanding Korean Honorifics (존댓말) for Beginners

Beginner guide · 6 min read

One of the biggest culture shocks for new Korean learners isn't vocabulary or grammar — it's realizing that the same sentence can be said in several different ways depending on who you're talking to. This system is called honorifics, and understanding the basics will save you a lot of confusion.

존댓말 vs. 반말: the two speech levels you'll hear most

Korean speech generally falls into two broad categories:

  • 존댓말 (jondaetmal) — polite/formal speech. Used with strangers, older people, teachers, bosses, customers, or anyone you don't know well. Verbs typically end in -요 or -ㅂ니다/습니다.
  • 반말 (banmal) — casual speech. Used only between close friends of a similar age, or by an older person speaking to someone clearly younger. Verb endings are shorter, without -요.
먹어요 (jondaetmal) vs. 먹어 (banmal)
Both mean "eat / I eat," but the first is polite and the second is casual.

Why this matters more in Korean than in English

English marks politeness mostly through word choice ("Could you help me?" vs. "Help me") and tone of voice. Korean marks it directly in the grammar — the verb ending itself changes. This means you can't just translate an English sentence word-for-word; you have to decide the correct speech level first, then build the sentence around it.

A simple rule while you're a beginner

Default to 존댓말 with everyone until you're explicitly invited to speak more casually — a close friend might say "우리 반말 할까?" ("should we speak casually?"). Using 존댓말 too much is never considered rude; using 반말 with the wrong person can be. When in doubt, stay polite.

It's not just about age

Beginners often assume honorifics are purely about age, but social context matters just as much. A younger store employee will still use 존댓말 with a customer their own age, and coworkers who are peers often stick to polite speech in professional settings regardless of age. Relationship and context, not just age, decide the right speech level.

How to practice this as a learner

The good news: almost every phrase on this site's daily phrase list is already in polite 존댓말 form, so you can use them safely in nearly any situation without worrying about sounding rude. As you progress, pay attention to how the ending of a sentence changes between dramas aimed at close friends versus workplace or customer-service settings — you'll start to notice the pattern quickly.
